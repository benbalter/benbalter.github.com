:quote[As open source projects grow and mature, maintainers' most pressing challenges often shift from the technical to the administrative as they transition from managing code to managing a community of contributors.]{#code-to-community} When I was responsible for Community and Safety at GitHub, I advised countless organizations on not just how to manage open source projects, but how to manage open source projects *at scale*.

Following my rule that [everything should have a URL](/2015/11/12/why-urls/), I've captured some of the most common advice I offered over the years into a series of four posts on successfully managing large open source communities:

1. [Set open source contributors up for success](/2020/05/15/set-open-source-contributors-up-for-success/)
2. [Automate common open source community management tasks](/2020/08/10/automate-common-open-source-community-management-tasks/)
3. [Practical tips for governing your open source project](/2021/06/14/open-source-governance/)
4. [Moderating open source conversations to keep them productive](/2021/06/15/how-to-moderate-open-source-conversations-to-keep-them-productive/)

If you (or your organization) are new to open source, these more "vintage" resources might help:

- [Tips for growing communities around your open source project](/2017/11/10/twelve-tips-for-growing-communities-around-your-open-source-project/)
- [Internal collaboration best practices](/2015/03/08/open-source-best-practices-internal-collaboration/)
- [External engagement best practices](/2015/03/17/open-source-best-practices-external-engagement/)

Last, if you have questions about intellectual property, I've also captured some more tactical advice around [open source licensing](/2017/11/28/everything-an-open-source-maintainer-might-need-to-know-about-open-source-licensing/) (including why you shouldn't [write your own license](/2016/08/01/why-you-shouldnt-write-your-own-open-source-license/)), [copyright notices](/2015/06/03/copyright-notices-for-websites-and-open-source-projects/), and [why you shouldn't require a contributor license agreement](/2018/01/02/why-you-probably-shouldnt-add-a-cla-to-your-open-source-project/). And if you (or someone in your organization) are still on the fence about open source, there are [plenty of reasons to consume, publish, and contribute to it](/2015/11/23/why-open-source/).

Start with the first post, and come back to the rest as your community outgrows what you have.
